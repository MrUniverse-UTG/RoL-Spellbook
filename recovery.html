Study of Recovery

Become a fantastic medic and support caster, healing wounds and mending broken bodies.

12 spells

Transfer Life

Rank 0
Power LevelNone
RangeTouch
Cast TimeStandard
DurationInstant
FocusNo
ComponentsH, T

Target: Cast on a creature within range.

Effect: You sacrifice up to 10 HP. The target creature recovers HP equal to the HP you sacrificed.

Limitation: You cannot cast this on creatures that are at 0 HP. You also can’t sacrifice life that would bring you to less than 10 HP.

Enhanced Study

When you gain certain Ranks in this Study, you gain additional benefits.

Rank 3: You can sacrifice up to 20 HP.

Rank 4: You can sacrifice up to 30 HP.

Rank 5: You can now cast this on creatures that are at 0 HP.

Deny Death

Rank 5
Power Level0
RangeTouch
Cast TimeStandard
DurationInstant
FocusNo
ComponentsH, T

Target: Cast on a corpse that hasn’t been dead for longer than a week.

Spell Check: You can perform a Spell check upon casting to increase the Power Level of the Spell.

Effect: If this Spell has at least 1 Power Level, you revive the body and return its soul to it. The revived body regains half its max HP, regains half its max MP, and cleanses the Wound, Trauma, or Doom Stack that killed it.

Failure: If this Spell fails to cast due to not having at least 1 Power Level, the corpse turns to ash. No one can cast this Spell on it again.

Willing: If the soul is not willing to return to the body, the Spell fails.

Limitations: If the corpse has been decapitated, the Spell fails.

Battle Fate: Death is not keen on losing a soul that it could have claimed and decides to stack fate against you in the future. If this Spell is cast on a corpse that was revived once by this Spell already, Death actively intervenes. The GM rolls 10 Base dice. Each success on these Base dice lowers the Power Level of this Spell by 1 to a minimum of 0.

Angered Fate: If this Spell is cast on a corpse that was revived by this Spell already, all the Base dice the GM rolls to lower the Power Level turn into Proficiency dice.
